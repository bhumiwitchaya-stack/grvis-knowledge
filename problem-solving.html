Diagnostic chain

Measured symptom → bounded problem statement (where/when/what/how much) → competing failure mechanisms → discriminating evidence → verified cause → containment → permanent correction → validation under representative conditions → monitoring for recurrence.

8D

D1 cross-functional team; D2 quantified description; D3 containment; D4 root cause and escape cause; D5 select corrective action; D6 implement and validate; D7 prevention/system update; D8 closure and lessons. Some organizations use D0 as preparation.

FMEA

Document failure mode, local/next-level/end effect, cause, controls, severity, occurrence, detection, owner and residual risk. Priority scores do not replace severity judgment; safety-critical failures can outrank high-frequency minor losses. Apply the organization's controlled FMEA standard where available.

Competing hypotheses

Example: intermittent servo position drift may arise from feedback noise, mechanical slip, drive configuration, grounding, cable damage or process disturbance. A mechanism becomes confirmed only when measurements differentiate it from plausible alternatives. A change in error rate alone may not prove causal correction.

Decision and verification

For each candidate remedy: predicted mechanism, benefit, side effects, resources, rollback, owner, measurement method, acceptance limit and verification window. Evidence provenance and instrumentation calibration are essential. Software/computer controls are preferred for deterministic acceptance checks.
